# Tirana public realm: seating, trees and building streaming

## What changed

- The central square's architectural reference distinguishes open paving from the planted perimeter. Preserve mapped trunks and remove only unverified estimated trunks inside nine named source garden polygons. The visual filter keeps the original mapping and footprints intact.
- The square gardens contained serial rows of closely spaced seats that the game drew as full-size repeated park benches. The shared game renderer keeps a subset with 10 m spacing at the square and 14 m along the nearest 46 m of the Lana gameplay alignment. Unrelated mapped benches and bus-stop seating are unchanged; both legacy road-decor paths skip guessed benches inside these areas. Standalone park fallback seating follows the same rule; playgrounds are exempt.
- Both games can now show the nearest complete building footprint batches without waiting for the rest of each 960 m cell. Actual courtyards and unloaded territory are not filled with fabricated blocks.

## Evidence and limits

- Skanderbeg Square project author, 51N4E: <https://51n4e.com/projects/skanderbeg-square/>.
- Primary local-government reference to public landscaped areas by Bulevardi Bajram Curri: <https://tirana.al/njesia-administrative/njesia-nr-1/info>. The game filter follows the existing source-frame river path, including its pre-existing interpolated portions. The density targets are presentation choices, not a new field inventory.
- Mapped city snapshot, © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL: <https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright>. The source snapshot reports 61 square-area seating points and 58 Lana-area points; the game shows 25 and 36 of these respectively before any race-specific visibility limits. All 3,388 unrelated points are preserved. The vegetation change removes 14 estimated points and no mapped trunk.
- In building-streaming probes using existing real footprints, near geometry first appeared at step 17 rather than 160 in the west and 147 in the east, with the same total number of steps and final batches. These are deterministic worker steps, not real-phone loading times.
