# Tirana Streets — asset sources

Tirana Streets is an original city sandbox. No Grand Theft Auto code, characters, branding, map or assets are used.

The character, city modules and asphalt material are reused from TonPlaygram's existing Tennis Royal / Kart Royale asset pipeline. The Kenney Car Kit was downloaded from the author's own download link. Cars retain the author's shared palette texture and geometry; renderer scaling and material response adapt them to meters and sunset lighting.

The GLB city asset contains two Quaternius buildings and their simplified meshes with embedded PBR maps, as previously prepared for Kart Royale. Repeated city details use GPU instancing. Most background building shells follow OSM footprints with authored facade details and inferred heights where map height tags are missing.

The Pyramid, Clock Tower, mosque and civic square meshes are original artistic reconstructions. The Pyramid's stepped exterior and colorful pavilion concept was referenced against [MVRDV's completed project](https://www.mvrdv.com/projects/312/the-pyramid-of-tirana). No architect photographs are redistributed. Facades, heights, road widths, traffic and missions are approximations for gameplay. This is a bounded central district, not a surveyed digital twin of the whole city.

Engine sound and mission cues are synthesized by the game; no sampled music or commercial game audio is included.

See `DATA-LICENSE.md`, the adjacent author license files and `sources.json` for provenance and checksums. CC0 terms: https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/

## Original street fixtures

`street-furniture.glb` is generated by `webapp/scripts/generate-tirana-street-furniture.mjs` from original TonPlaygram geometry: three branched/leafed tree variants, traffic-light housing, road/street signs, lamps, benches, bins, bollards, tree grates and drains. No additional third-party models or runtime model CDN are used for these fixtures. The generator is kept with the project source; rebuild it through the existing predev/prebuild step. Street names derive from the attributed OSM extract.

## Blender buildings and landscape — September 2026 update

Four original facade archetypes and four distance LODs were authored, exported and CPU-rendered in **Blender 4.5.3 LTS** using `scripts/build-tirana-blender.py`. The editable source is `art/tirana-streets/tirana-buildings.blend`. The runtime is `tirana-buildings.glb` (about 12 MB). Balcony rails, frames/reveals, floor ledges, parapets, shopfront glazing, planters, drainpipes and rooftop equipment are geometry. Nearby fitted OSM footprints use these models; large footprints are divided into adjoining modules to limit stretched window dimensions. They are architectural approximations, not one-to-one surveyed buildings.

| Material | Author/source | License | Use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Plastered Wall 02 | [Charlotte Baglioni / Poly Haven](https://polyhaven.com/a/plastered_wall_02) | CC0 | 2K diffuse, OpenGL normal and roughness; walls and concrete banks |
| Grass Path 2 | [Rob Tuytel / Poly Haven](https://polyhaven.com/a/grass_path_2) | CC0 | 2K diffuse, OpenGL normal and roughness; mixed soil/grass terrain beneath modeled grass tufts |

`materials/sources.json` records local files and checksums. The river's animated ripples and grass blades are original procedural geometry/shader work. No Google imagery, 3D tiles, or map textures are redistributed in these assets.

Google Maps Street View was inspected at Bajram Curri Boulevard (approximately 41.32395, 19.82190) in this session. The latest selectable capture at this specific point was **September 2025**, with October 2024 and December 2023 alternatives. Observations: dense broadleaf street trees, rectangular paving, low curbs, grassy channel banks, planted verges, shopfronts and mixed midrise facades. This does not establish the date or condition of every city block. Google Earth itself returned `WebGL is not supported on your browser`. Direct Google 3D streaming is not enabled; it requires the documented [Photorealistic 3D Tiles API](https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/tile/3d-tiles) and an authorized API key/billing project.

The western Lana reach missing from the existing waterway data is interpolated through mapped bridge centres. This is explicitly inferred geometry. Both renderer and collision simulation use the same channel. Road/foot bridge approaches and mapped pedestrian paths remain gaps in the railings.

Google 3D surroundings: conditional live Map Tiles API integration; no Google imagery is included in this source/asset archive. The local Draco decoder is Apache-2.0 (see draco/LICENSE). The original Blender city remains the active fallback.


## Continuous pavements and portrait controls — September 2026

`street-kit.glb` and `art/tirana-streets/tirana-street-kit.blend` contain original modeled urban fixtures and pavements triangulated around the attributed OSM road/building geometry. Road crossings have lowered, sloping curb approaches. Shelters, bicycle racks, utility cabinets, planters, hydrants, manholes and tactile tiles are fictional dressing, not surveyed fixture locations. Their placement and collision footprints are shared by the renderer and simulation.

- [Pavement 02](https://polyhaven.com/a/pavement_02): Charlotte Baglioni (photography), Dario Barresi (processing), Poly Haven, CC0. 2K diffuse/OpenGL normal; 1K roughness/AO. Paving UVs use a 2.1 m repeat.
- [Bark Brown 02](https://polyhaven.com/a/bark_brown_02): Rob Tuytel, Poly Haven, CC0. 1K diffuse/OpenGL normal/roughness. Trunk UVs use physical dimensions.

`materials/street-surfaces-sources.json` records the exact downloaded URLs, file sizes and SHA256. Original tree geometry now has separate close/distant glTF models; canopy motion is an original shader. The daylight sky and filtered reflection map are generated with Three.js Sky (MIT). These changes add no Google imagery or extracted Google geometry.

The final street GLB stores 4.4 MB of Draco geometry and references 9.2 MB of shared image files through relative glTF image URIs. Image bytes are unchanged; paver diffuse/normal JPEGs are reused directly, and the Blender-exported packed roughness PNG is retained. `materials/street-kit-runtime-sources.json` records their checksums. The compressed editable Blender source uses relative references to the versioned materials; open it within the repository. `scripts/externalize-street-textures.py` reproduces the lossless extraction after Blender export.
